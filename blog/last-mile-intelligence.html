Last-Mile Intelligence: When the Answer Has to Reach a Phone

Neolith Team · September 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Most business data is technically accessible and practically unreachable. The last mile is the difference between a dashboard someone opens and an answer that arrives.

Ask a warehouse supervisor for last month's return rate and you will get a number, eventually — after a dashboard, a spreadsheet export and a message to someone in the office. The data was never missing. The path to it was.

The last mile is a delivery problem

Most companies have already solved data collection. The gap is between where data sits and where decisions get made: on a shop floor, in a van, at a client site, standing up. Screens assume attention that these moments do not have. What works instead is a question asked in plain language and answered in a sentence, on the device already in the person's hand.

Unification comes before conversation

You cannot query what you have not joined. Before an agent can answer "which of our top accounts have an overdue delivery?", someone has to decide that customer, order and shipment mean the same thing across three systems. That work is unglamorous — naming conventions, one canonical identifier per entity, a decision about which source wins when two disagree — and it is the actual prerequisite. A natural-language layer over inconsistent data does not produce insight; it produces confident nonsense.

Two habits make unification survivable. Model the handful of entities your questions actually concern, rather than attempting enterprise-wide harmony. And record provenance, so every answer can point at the system it came from — which is what makes the answer trustworthy rather than merely fluent.

Guardrails for answers that travel

  • Scoped access. The agent inherits the asker's permissions, not the database's. A question asked in a group chat should never return numbers that person could not otherwise see.
  • Read before write. Start with queries that only retrieve. Writes — adjusting an order, updating a record — come after the read path has earned trust.
  • Say what is unknown. "The last sync was four hours ago" is a useful answer. A plausible guess is not.

Design for the sixty-second window

These interactions happen in gaps: between two tasks, in a lift, at a traffic light. An answer that takes ninety seconds to compose has already lost. That constraint pushes the design toward narrow, well-indexed questions over anything resembling open-ended analysis — and toward knowing the most common questions before the system is built, which is exactly what the workflow work upstream gives you.

The test

The measure of last-mile intelligence is not how many questions the system can answer. It is whether someone who would otherwise have asked a colleague instead asked the system first, and got something they trusted enough to act on. That is an adoption question, not a modelling one — and it is why the unglamorous unification work is the part that decides whether this lands.
